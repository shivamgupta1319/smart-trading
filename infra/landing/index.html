Paper-trading lab, NSE

Every trade booked after real costs. Most strategies don't survive it.

Smart Trader runs 28 trading strategies on Indian stocks with pretend money. It books each trade with brokerage, taxes and slippage, measures it against the rupees it risked, and keeps score of which stock-and-strategy pairs actually earn.

What the audit found, and what changed

Two earlier versions ran in paper for months: 4,066 and 337 closed trades. Their live databases were queried line by line before anything was rebuilt.

Costs were not booked

FoundAbout ₹174 of charges per intraday trade, roughly ₹6.9 lakh in total, none of it recorded. The good-looking intraday results came from that.

NowEvery result is after brokerage, taxes and slippage, and a trade whose costs exceed a quarter of its risk is skipped.

Fills were too kind

FoundEntries booked at an old candle's close; stops booked at exactly the stop price.

NowEntries at the live price when the signal appears; stops at the worse of the stop and the live price; a gap through a stop fills at the open.

No limit on re-entry

FoundOne strategy traded about three times per stock per day: 1,557 trades, −₹6.56 lakh.

NowA 30-minute cooldown and at most 2 trades a day per intraday pair, in both scanner and backtest. That strategy and two others were removed.

Nothing switched losers off

FoundNo automatic pause in either version.

NowA pair pauses itself when half its fund is gone.

How it works

A cell is one stock with one strategy
Each cell has its own ₹10,000 of pretend money. Intraday trades may use five times that. No trade may risk more than 2% of what it may hold: ₹200 for a swing trade, ₹1,000 intraday.
The scanner trades in market hours
It checks every live cell, opens a paper trade when a strategy signals between 09:30 and 15:00, and closes it at the stop, the target, or 15:15 for intraday.
Results are counted in R
R is a trade's profit or loss divided by the rupees it risked: +1R made what it risked, −1R lost all of it. An average above zero after costs, over many trades, is the only number that matters.
Each cell earns its status
From its own paper trades, not its backtest. A cell is Proven after 40+ trades that are profitable after costs and positive in both halves, and Paused once half its fund is gone. TestingProvenPaused
A backtest only gets a pair in the door
Today's roster is 359 pairs on 187 stocks: liquid NIFTY 500 names for swing, a measured list of 38 fast-moving stocks for intraday. Only its own paper trades keep a pair there.
Telegram keeps the owner informed
Trade alerts and a Friday summary arrive on Telegram, so nothing has to be watched all day.

What the evidence says, plainly

Every strategy is backtested with the same entry rules and costs the live scanner uses. Testing 28 strategies at once means some will look good by luck, so the results are also corrected for that.

No intraday strategy shows an edge after costs. Several swing strategies look positive, and none has proved itself yet.

After correcting for 28 tries, eight swing strategies pass the first luck test. The stricter test, which asks whether the best result beats the best of all the lucky tries, passes none; Golden_Cross comes closest. These swing results are also flattered by a stock list chosen in 2026, and on a wider 471-stock run the averages drop (Golden_Cross +0.86R became +0.53R). Every swing strategy lost in live paper trading during the Aug–Oct 2026 fall.

So the plan is a fresh database and three to six months of paper trading, judged on live results, before any real money.

Real money: six locks, all of them needed

The app can place real orders at the Dhan broker. It does so only when every one of these is true, and today the first one isn't.

  1. The trading mode is set to live. It is off by default, and off now.
  2. The cell is Proven: 40+ paper trades, profitable after costs, positive in both halves.
  3. The owner has switched that cell on by hand.
  4. The stock's broker id is saved.
  5. The signal is intraday. Swing signals never trade real money.
  6. Today's limits on orders and daily loss are not used up.

Entries and exits are separate: anything that stops new real trades never stops an open real position from being closed.

How it's built, and its limits

Stack

  • Nx monorepo
  • NestJS + Prisma
  • Python FastAPI engine
  • React + Vite
  • PostgreSQL
  • Socket.io
  • Telegram
  • Docker on my own server

Strategies, backtests and the live scanner are in Python; trades, sizing, costs and cell status in the NestJS API. One settings file holds the fund size, leverage, risk cap and costs, and both read it, so the backtest and live trading can't drift apart.

Limits, stated plainly

  • Paper fills are still not real fills; a live order would see queues and partial fills.
  • A single pair's backtest is not evidence: of 381 pairs with 30+ trades, the number that pass a strict bar is about what chance alone would give.
  • The stock lists are chosen from today's liquid names, which favours stocks that went up.
  • Not investment advice.
